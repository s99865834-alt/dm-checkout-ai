# Instagram Outreach Strategy: Candle & Home Fragrance Brands

Goal: warm, founder-to-founder conversations that turn into installs, activated
stores, and eventually reviews. Motion: reply to stories organically, let them
respond, pitch only after a real exchange.

---

## 1. Why candles

- **The founder is the account.** One-person operations: the same person pours,
  packs, ships, and posts. A story reply lands directly with the decision-maker.
- **Daily story content.** Pouring videos, packing orders, restock countdowns.
  Constant organic openings for a reply that reads human, not spam.
- **Scent can't be photographed.** Their DMs fill with buying-intent questions:
  "what does this smell like?", "is it strong?", "when does fall restock?"
  Every unanswered one is a lost sale. That's the pitch, pre-written by their
  own customers.
- **Saturated market.** Thousands of candle brands, thin margins, high hunger
  to differentiate.

Backup vertical if candles stall after a genuine 2-week run: crystals /
spiritual goods (even higher DM volume, "DM to claim" culture).

---

## 2. Finding brands

1. **Supplier tagged-photos trick (best source).** Open the Instagram accounts
   of candle-making suppliers (CandleScience, Makesy, packaging suppliers) and
   browse their **tagged photos**. Everyone tagging a supplier is an active,
   serious candle business.
2. **Account-name search.** Search "candle co" in account search. The
   "[Name] Candle Co" naming pattern is nearly always founder-run.
3. **Hashtags, Recent tab.** #smallbatchcandles #handpouredcandles
   #candlemaker #candlebusiness. Brands actively hashtagging are actively
   trying to grow.
4. **Seed the algorithm.** Follow 10-15 good accounts. Use the "Suggested for
   you" carousel that appears right after tapping Follow. Within two days,
   Explore becomes a lead feed.

## 3. Qualification checklist (30 seconds per account)

- [ ] 5k-100k followers (big enough to drown in DMs, small enough that the
      founder answers them personally)
- [ ] Stories active in the last 24 hours
- [ ] Bio link goes to a real store: own domain or Shopify-looking URL.
      **Etsy-only sellers can't install the app - skip.**
- [ ] Bio reads founder-operated: "hi, I'm Sarah", "handmade by me"
- [ ] Bonus: unanswered buying-intent comments ("how much?", "still
      available?") sitting on recent posts - doubles as the pitch later

Save qualified accounts to a saved collection ("candles") and work through it.

---

## 4. Daily reps (sustainable pace)

- Qualify **15 accounts** into the collection
- Genuinely engage with **10** (story replies beat comments for starting
  conversations)
- Open **3-5 real conversations**

Track per week in a simple note: story replies sent → responses → conversations
→ pitches → installs. The ratios tell you what to fix (no responses = better
story choice; responses but failed pitches = better transition).

## 5. The story-reply playbook

**Reply to craft stories, not promo stories.** A reply to a pouring /
scent-testing / order-packing story reads as a person; a reply to a sale story
reads as a customer or a pitch.

- Keep the first reply 100% genuine, zero agenda: react to the craft, ask what
  wax/wick/scent that is, comment on the packaging.
- If they respond, be a normal person for 2-4 exchanges. No product talk.
- Transition (only after real back-and-forth). The finalized go-to line:

  > "you clearly put a lot of thought into how the brand comes across! are you
  > the one answering all the DMs and comments too? 😅 i build a tool in this
  > space so i'm always nosy how brands handle it"

  Why it works: the question is about *who* does it (logistics, safe), never
  *whether it's done well* (quality, stings). "All" plants the workload idea
  without claiming it. The "i build a tool / always nosy" clause is disclosure
  disguised as curiosity, so the later pitch never feels like an ambush.

  Candle-specific alternative when there's visible evidence on their grid:

  > "saw a couple 'what does it smell like?' comments on your last drop - do
  > you handle all those yourself? i build a tool in this space so i'm always
  > curious how brands keep up"

## 6. The pitch (control-first framing)

Lesson from past rejections ("we're steering clear of automated stuff"): the
automation objection forms *before* you mention control. So lead with control.

Template (adapt, don't paste):

> love that, the personal touch matters. I built a Shopify app for exactly
> this - it replies to DMs + comments in your brand voice and drops checkout
> links so convos convert while you're pouring. you keep full control: it only
> runs on posts you pick, replies in your voice, off anytime.

Offer (no-risk close):

> happy to give you 6 months free. worst case you delete it, best case it's a
> revenue stream you didn't have to hire for 🤝

Revenue framing when useful: every "what does this smell like?" that sits
unanswered overnight is a sale that walked. The app closes them 24/7 and shows
the exact revenue it drove.

## 7. Rules of engagement

- One last-ditch reply after a "no", then let it go. No follow-up after that.
  Stay warm - they may come back during holiday-season DM overload (Q4 is the
  candle industry's crunch; a brand that says no in August may say yes in
  October).
- Rejected leads stay in the collection. Re-engage naturally (story reply,
  comment) after ~2 months, never with another pitch message.
- Never send the pitch as the first message. The whole edge of this motion is
  that it doesn't look like the spam they already get.
- Reviews: never tie the free offer to a review. Give free access as a
  founder helping test. Ask for an honest review separately, ~1-2 weeks after
  they're actively using it (Shopify policy 1.3 prohibits incentivized
  reviews; detailed reviews from active stores also weigh most).

## 8. When someone installs

1. Install alert email fires (new-install alert on afterAuth).
2. Same-day personal onboarding: offer to walk them through connecting
   Instagram (the activation moment).
3. Watch that they map their top posts and the AI answers a real DM within the
   first week.
4. Week 1-2, once they've seen value: the review ask. Hand them ingredients,
   not a blank box: "even 2-3 sentences helps - what you sell, what it's doing
   for you, how setup was."
